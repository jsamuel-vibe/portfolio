Long story short

The project changed once the original reason for building it fell apart.

01

Forced reset

Cretile lost access to its Meta account. A new number meant rebuilding the campaign setup anyway.

02

Wrong assumption

I believed our provider was adding roughly ₹3,500 to every campaign. After development began, I learned Meta was charging it.

03

New direction

The cost argument disappeared, but a larger opportunity remained: a system Cretile could shape around its own sales workflow.

The situation

A lost account created a chance to reconsider the whole setup.

I ran Cretile’s WhatsApp campaigns. Lead data lived in Excel, so each campaign began with another export as new contacts were added.

Interakt itself was not difficult to use. The question was whether reconnecting a new number to another monthly tool was the right long-term decision.

The assumption that failed

I started building around a business case I had misunderstood.

With roughly 4,000 leads, I believed building internally would remove about ₹3,500 in campaign charges.

→

Then I checked the pricing more carefully with Claude: Meta charged for the messages, not Interakt.

Building our own interface would not make that cost disappear.

That left an uncomfortable question: if the product was not meaningfully cheaper, why should Cretile own it?

Reframing the product

Ownership would matter only if the system could behave differently.

Not enough

Recreate Interakt

The same campaign workflow in a product we owned was not a meaningful advantage.

The opportunity

Build around Cretile

Treat WhatsApp as the first working module of one system for leads, campaigns, replies, ownership, qualification and follow-up.

What shipped in two weeks

A working campaign and inbox system—not the whole platform.

I already understood the operator workflow because I created the campaigns myself. The first version focused on the parts required to run them safely.

Contacts and tags Campaign creation Message preview Audience protection Number warm-up Inbox and analytics
Selected design decisions

Most of the design work was about preventing expensive mistakes.

01

Tag at the point of entry

Every imported contact needs a tag, so an audience already has structure when a campaign is created.

02

See the message before sending

The message changes beside the form, letting the operator check what a person will receive before continuing.

03

Opt-outs stay locked

A person who asks to stop remains visible for accountability but cannot be accidentally added back.

04

A few seconds to undo

Sending waits briefly so the operator can catch the wrong audience or a typo.

05

Make the reply window visible

The inbox shows whether WhatsApp’s 24-hour free-reply window is still open, so the salesperson knows when a normal response is allowed.

06

Warm the number without babysitting it

Sending starts slowly, increases over time and stops if Meta reports a warning.

Where ownership became useful

Claude watched the replies when I could not.

In one campaign, I asked people to choose between four kinds of support. Each answer needed a different follow-up.

Instead of watching the inbox and opening every conversation, I asked Claude to monitor the replies and send the matching response.

≈40 option-based follow-ups handled while I was away
What exists today

A live product with two real roles—and an honest limitation.

Campaign operator

I add contacts, create campaigns and run them.

Salesperson

One salesperson uses the inbox to reply.

The limitation

The basic campaign workflow is not yet clearly better than Interakt.

The most promising advantage is the ability to create Cretile-specific automations because Cretile owns the system.

Proposed direction · Not yet built

From website enquiry to a qualified conversation

The next opportunity is not another campaign feature. It is connecting the full sales journey. I developed this direction by examining our workflow, speaking with the salesperson and founders, exploring it with Claude and aligning the team around a shared structure.

What changed in my thinking

Owning a product is not an advantage by itself.

I should have verified the pricing model before I started building. But being wrong exposed a better test for the product: ownership is useful only when it lets the system behave differently.

The most meaningful difference so far did not come from rebuilding the campaign screens. It came when Claude became part of the product workflow itself.
