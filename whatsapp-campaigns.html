A WhatsApp campaign tool, built in two weeks

I pitched, designed and built my company’s WhatsApp campaign tool on my own. It replaced a paid tool, and the marketing team now uses it for every campaign.

The problem

We were paying ₹1,000 a month for an outside WhatsApp campaign tool. I pitched building our own, then designed and built it.

Limits

#1

Two weeks, no more. We had already cancelled the old tool, so we couldn’t send any campaigns until mine was ready.

#2

Just me. No developers and no budget. Design, frontend and backend were all mine.

#3

My day job didn’t stop. I still had all my marketing work to do.

What I built, what I cut

I listed what the team really needed and cut everything else, so all two weeks went into the parts that mattered.

Built
  • Uploading contacts
  • Creating campaigns
  • Inbox
Cut
  • Multiple user roles
  • Analytics
  • Visual polish
  • Automations

What I decided

01 Every upload needs a tag

You have to tag contacts when you upload them. That way they’re already grouped when you choose who gets a campaign.

02 See the message before you send it

You build a campaign step by step, with a live preview next to the form. You see exactly what people will get.

03 Opted-out contacts are locked

Before sending, the tool shows who is left out and why. People who opted out are locked and can’t be added back, so no one gets a message after asking to stop.

04 A few seconds to undo

Nothing goes out the moment you press Send. A countdown appears with an Undo button, so there’s time to catch the wrong audience or a typo.

05 The inbox shows who you can still reply to

WhatsApp only lets you reply freely within 24 hours of someone’s last message. The inbox shows that window on each chat, so the team knows who they can still answer.

06 Send slowly to stay safe

Sending thousands of messages at once can get a number blocked by Meta. So the tool starts at 200 messages a day and steps up slowly, spreads sends across the day, and stops on its own if Meta sends a warning. It all runs by itself, so nobody has to manage it.

What I learned

Three lessons from a hard deadline.

#1

Finish first, polish later

I had to stop judging the screens by how they look. I still don’t like the UI, and I know it can be better. But holding back is the only reason it shipped on time.

#2

Plan the time

I now set a timeline at the start of every project. Each part gets enough time, and I don’t overdo any of it like I did with Superstash.

#3

Write things down

I didn’t write down my thinking or the use cases I had in mind while designing. I wish I had.

Where it is now

It’s the company’s main campaign tool, and I still run every campaign on it.
